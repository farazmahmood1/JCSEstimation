The main responsibility of an estimating contractor is to prepare accurate cost estimates for construction projects, which can help clients determine the feasibility of a project and make informed decisions about budgeting and financing.

An estimate contractor typically works with clients such as developers, architects, engineers, and general contractors. They may be hired at various stages of a construction project, from the initial planning phase to the bidding process and beyond. Estimating contractors may work independently or as an employee of a larger construction company.

Estimate contractor needs to have a strong understanding of the construction industry, including knowledge of materials, labor costs, and building codes and regulations. They should also have strong analytical and mathematical skills to develop accurate cost estimates.

8 Basic Skills an estimate contractor needs to have:

  1. Knowledge of the construction industry: Estimate contractors should have a solid understanding of the construction industry, including materials, labor costs, and building codes and regulations.
  2. Technical skills: Estimating contractors need strong mathematical and analytical skills to develop accurate cost estimates. They should be proficient in using computer software for estimating and project management.
  3. Attention to detail: The ability to pay close attention to details is critical for estimating contractors. Even small errors in estimates can result in significant cost overruns.
  4. Communication skills: Estimating contractors need to be effective communicators, able to explain complex information to clients and other stakeholders. They should be able to listen actively, ask questions, and provide clear, concise information.
  5. Time management skills: Estimating contractors often work on multiple projects simultaneously, so they need strong time management skills to meet deadlines and prioritize tasks.
  6. Negotiation skills: Estimating contractors may be involved in the bidding process for construction projects. They should be able to negotiate effectively with clients, contractors, and suppliers to get the best prices for materials and services.
  7. Problem-solving skills: Estimating contractors should be able to identify potential problems and find solutions to ensure that projects stay on track and within budget.
  8. Teamwork: An estimate contractor often works as part of a team, so they should be able to collaborate effectively with other professionals in the construction industry, including architects, engineers, and general contractors.

6 Reasons why estimating contractors is Important for the Industry

  • Budgeting and Financing: Accurate cost estimates are essential for budgeting and financing a construction project. An estimate contractor helps clients determine the feasibility of a project by providing a detailed breakdown of costs, including labor, materials, and other expenses.
  • Project Planning: Cost estimates provided by estimating contractors are used for project planning, helping clients to identify potential cost savings and make informed decisions about scheduling and resource allocation.
  • Risk Management: Estimating contractors helps identify and mitigate potential risks associated with a construction project. They can anticipate cost overruns, schedule delays, and other challenges.
  • Bidding Process: Estimating contractors are often involved in the bidding process for construction projects, helping clients prepare competitive bids and negotiate with suppliers and contractors.
  • Quality Control: Accurate cost estimates provided by estimating contractors ensure that construction projects are completed to the required standards of quality, and within the set budget.
  • Cost Control: An estimate contractor helps clients to control costs throughout the construction process. They monitor actual costs against estimated costs and adjust estimates accordingly, ensuring that the project stays within the set budget.

Who can be an estimated contractor?

  • Education: An estimate contractor has a degree in construction management, engineering, or a related field.
  • Experience: Many estimating contractors start their careers as construction workers or project managers.
  • Apprenticeships: Some estimating contractors start as apprentices, working under the guidance of experienced contractors.
  • Certification: Some organizations offer certification programs for estimate contractors.
